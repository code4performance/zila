# Code of Conduct

Brasa is a project about making programming clearer and more welcoming. The community around it should be the same. This code applies to everyone who takes part: contributors, maintainers, people who only open an issue, and anyone who speaks for the project in public.

## Our standard

We want Brasa to be a place where people of any background feel able to contribute and learn. That includes every level of experience, nationality, language, gender, identity, age, ability and belief.

**What we ask of everyone**

- **Be respectful.** Criticize ideas, code and decisions, never people. Disagreement is healthy; contempt is not.
- **Be welcoming to beginners.** Everyone started somewhere. Answer questions kindly and patiently, and do not make anyone feel stupid for asking.
- **Be constructive.** When something is wrong, say why and, when you can, suggest how to fix it.
- **Assume good faith, and check your own tone.** Text hides tone. If a message could be read two ways, ask before reacting.
- **Be patient.** Maintainers and reviewers are volunteers with other lives. Language is also a barrier: many of us write in a second language, so be generous with clumsy phrasing.
- **Credit others.** Say where an idea or a piece of code came from.
- **Take responsibility.** If you make a mistake or hurt someone, acknowledge it, apologize and learn from it.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, slurs, personal attacks, or demeaning jokes.
- Discrimination of any kind.
- Threats, intimidation, stalking, or publishing someone's private information.
- Sexualized language or images, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Sustained disruption: derailing discussions, flooding, or repeatedly re-opening a decided question without new arguments.
- Passing off other people's work as your own, or submitting code you are not allowed to share.
- Deliberately submitting harmful code, or trying to trick the project into shipping it.

Strong technical opinions are fine. Rudeness is not.

## Scope

This code applies in all project spaces: the repository, issues, pull requests, comments, and any official channel. It also applies when someone is clearly acting as a representative of the project elsewhere.

## Reporting a concern

If you see or experience behavior that breaks this code, or you have a security concern that should not be public, tell the maintainers **privately**. Use the contact details on the GitHub profile of the repository owner (`code4performance`), or open an issue that says only that you need a private contact, without describing the problem, and a maintainer will reach out.

Reports are handled confidentially. The maintainers will not reveal who reported something without their permission, and retaliation against anyone who reports in good faith is itself a violation.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for applying this code, fairly and in proportion. Depending on what happened, they may:

1. **Talk to you**, privately or in the thread, and ask for a change in behavior.
2. **Give a warning** in writing, explaining what was wrong and what comes next.
3. **Remove or edit** comments, issues or commits that break this code.
4. **Suspend** your participation for a stated period.
5. **Remove you** from the project permanently, for serious or repeated violations.

Whenever possible the person involved is told what was decided and why. Maintainers who are involved in a report step aside from deciding it; if the lead maintainer is involved, the other maintainers decide. If you think a decision was wrong, you can ask for it to be reviewed by writing to a maintainer who was not involved.

## Changes to this code

Like the rest of the project, this code can improve. Changes follow the amendment process in the [Project Charter](GOVERNANCE.md#amending-this-charter).
